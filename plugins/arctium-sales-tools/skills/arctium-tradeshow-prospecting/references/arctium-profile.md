# Arctium Energy — company profile & fit framework

Use this to judge whether an exhibitor/attendee at an event is worth pursuing, and to write the internal "Angle" note for each one.

## What Arctium sells

Arctium Energy is a BC-based company offering three connected things:

- **BESS** — battery energy storage systems, sized for commercial & industrial (C&I) sites. Use cases: peak shaving, demand response, backup power / resilience, and firming for sites with constrained or expensive grid interconnection.
- **EV charging** — infrastructure including off-grid/mobile chargers and DC fast chargers, often paired with on-site storage so a site can add charging without an expensive grid upgrade.
- **DataTrack** — an AI-driven energy monitoring platform for real-time tracking of energy (and sometimes water/gas) usage across a site or portfolio.

## Who actually buys, and how

Two buyer motions, don't conflate them:

1. **Direct sale** — an organization that owns/operates facilities with a real peak-demand, backup-power, or grid-capacity problem (a port, a campus, a cold-storage warehouse, a data center, a rail yard, a housing portfolio). The pitch is a conversation about *their* site.
2. **Channel / ecosystem** — an organization that doesn't buy BESS itself but sits between Arctium and buyers: an industry association whose members are buyers, an engineering/consulting firm that specifies equipment for clients, a general contractor or equipment dealer who could bundle storage into what they already sell, or a funding/financing body whose grants could pay for a buyer's storage project. The pitch here is partnership, not a sale — membership, referral, co-pitch, or a resource/education contribution.

Sorting exhibitors into these two buckets up front changes how you'll frame the email later (see the outreach-emails skill).

## Category patterns that have worked

These aren't exhaustive — use judgment for the specific event — but they're the recurring shapes seen across events so far (trade shows, energy conferences, construction/infrastructure events):

- **Utilities / energy program operators** — direct sales + channel (they often run their own customer-facing efficiency/storage programs Arctium could plug into as an approved vendor).
- **Ports, terminals, rail, industrial sites** — direct sales. Electrifying heavy equipment fleets is usually the trigger; storage avoids a grid upgrade.
- **Campuses / institutions** (colleges, hospitals, government facilities, training centers) — direct sales, usually via a sustainability or facilities role.
- **Housing / real estate** (housing authorities, multi-res developers, REITs, retrofit programs) — direct sales, usually via an energy/sustainability manager.
- **Data centers / hyperscale / colocation** — direct sales for backup power and grid-capacity relief, though hyperscalers often run their own large-scale power programs; smaller/regional operators and their construction partners are a better first wedge than the hyperscalers themselves.
- **General contractors / EPC / trade contractors building the above** — channel. They're already on-site installing power infrastructure; storage can be a bundled scope item.
- **Engineering & consulting firms** (electrical, mechanical, energy audits, decarbonization consulting) — channel. They specify equipment for clients and are a near-perfect referral/co-delivery partner.
- **Industry & trade associations** — channel. Low direct-sale probability, high value as a credibility/awareness channel to reach many buyers through one relationship (membership, sponsorship, a session, a resource).
- **Equipment dealers** (construction/heavy equipment, forestry, mining gear) — channel. Their customers are electrifying fleets; storage + charging is a natural bundle with equipment sales.
- **Funding / financing / grant bodies** — channel. They can't buy storage, but they can fund a buyer's project or connect Arctium to grantees.
- **Grid operators / regulators** (ISOs, utilities' system-planning arms) — ecosystem/awareness. Not a sales target, but relevant to understand and reference in outreach to others at the same event (e.g. "given the interconnection constraints [grid operator] has flagged...").

## What NOT to do

- Don't force-fit every exhibitor. A vendor of, say, office furniture or catering at a trade show is not a target — skip it rather than write a strained "Angle."
- Don't treat hyperscalers/very large operators as if they were a small business — their power infrastructure decisions go through internal teams and long procurement cycles. Still worth a first-touch email, but the "Angle" should acknowledge scale rather than pretend Arctium is a first-time introduction to storage.
- Don't skip an organization just because it's not an obvious C&I buyer — associations, consultants, and financing bodies are often higher-leverage than a single direct sale.
